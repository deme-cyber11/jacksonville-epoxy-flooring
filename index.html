Epoxy That Peels Isn't an Epoxy Problem — It's a Prep Problem

If you've seen epoxy floors peel in Florida — bubbling up in patches, lifting at the edges, separating from the concrete after one summer — you've seen what happens when the moisture issue isn't addressed before the coating goes down.

Jacksonville's high humidity and temperature extremes require moisture-vapor barrier prep before any epoxy install — skipping this step is the #1 reason epoxy peels in Florida. Concrete in Northeast Florida is humid concrete. It transmits moisture vapor from the ground up through the slab constantly. A coating applied over that without proper vapor barrier prep will eventually fail — sometimes in months, sometimes in a year or two, but it will fail.

The companies competing on price often skip the moisture testing and proper barrier installation because it adds cost and time. The result is a floor that looks great for the first season and then starts delaminating.

We've been cleaning up those failures in Jacksonville, Mandarin, Ponte Vedra, and surrounding Northeast Florida for years. And we've watched homeowners pay twice — once for the cheap job and once to fix it right.

Why Epoxy Fails in Florida — and How to Make Sure It Doesn't

  • Moisture vapor transmission (MVT) Jacksonville's concrete slabs transmit moisture vapor at higher rates than most of the country. Proper testing before installation, followed by a moisture-vapor barrier primer, is what prevents delamination. Ask any contractor you're considering: how do you test for MVT and what barrier system do you use? A vague answer is a red flag.
  • Temperature during application Epoxy applied in direct Florida summer heat — concrete surface temperatures of 90°F+ — cures too fast, trapping bubbles and preventing full adhesion. Professional installation requires temperature monitoring and timing of application windows.
  • Surface preparation depth Diamond grinding opens the concrete pores for chemical bonding. Acid etching alone (cheaper and faster) creates inconsistent surface profiles — epoxy adhesion is dramatically lower. This shortcut is very common in low-bid installations.
  • Salt air in coastal communities Properties in Ponte Vedra, Jacksonville Beach, and other coastal areas face an additional corrosion factor: salt air. It accelerates concrete deterioration and creates unique adhesion challenges. Our barrier system addresses this specifically for salt air exposure zones.

How It Works

Our 5-Step Florida-Grade Installation Process

What separates a floor that lasts 15 years from one that peels in 18 months is entirely in the preparation. Here's exactly what we do.

1

Free On-Site Assessment

Day 1 - Morning

We inspect the concrete, check for existing coatings, note any cracks or damage, and test moisture vapor transmission (MVT). MVT testing is non-negotiable in Florida — it determines the barrier system required. Written quote provided before we leave.

2

Diamond Grinding & Repair

Day 1

Diamond grinding (not acid etching) profiles the concrete surface for maximum mechanical bonding. Cracks and spalls are filled. Any existing peeling coatings are fully removed. This step takes longer than cheap competitors budget for — it's why our jobs don't fail.

3

Moisture-Vapor Barrier Primer

Day 1 - Late

A moisture-vapor barrier primer is applied and allowed to fully cure before any topcoat. This is the step most peeling jobs skipped. We never skip it. Cure time is monitored and we don't proceed until the barrier has properly set.

4

Epoxy Coating System

Day 2

Base coat, color/flake/metallic layer, and protective topcoat applied with proper temperature monitoring. In Florida, concrete surface temp matters — we time application windows to avoid peak heat that causes adhesion failures and trapped bubbles.

5

Final Inspection & Warranty

Day 3 - Morning

Full walkthrough with you before we consider the job complete. Any touch-up or edge work addressed on the spot. 15-year warranty documentation signed and provided. Light foot traffic in 24 hours, vehicles in 72 hours.

Why Jacksonville Epoxy Flooring Is Different From the Rest of the Country

Jacksonville's high humidity and temperature extremes require moisture-vapor barrier prep before any epoxy install — skipping this step is the #1 reason epoxy peels in Florida. This isn't a marketing statement — it's what we see in the field every time we're called in to fix a failed installation:

Concrete moisture content

Florida's water table and seasonal rainfall keep concrete slabs at higher moisture levels than virtually any other region. We test every slab before installation — if MVT is above threshold, a barrier system is required before topcoat. Period.

Thermal cycling

Jacksonville concrete gets hot. A garage floor in July can hit 120°F+ surface temperature in direct sun. Epoxy applied or cured in those conditions fails faster. We schedule work for appropriate temperature windows and specify products rated for subtropical climates.

Salt air exposure in coastal neighborhoods

Mandarin, Ponte Vedra, Fernandina Beach, and Jacksonville Beach properties are in salt air exposure zones. Salt air accelerates concrete degradation and creates a hostile environment for standard epoxy adhesion. We specify systems rated for coastal exposure for all properties within appropriate distance of the ocean.
