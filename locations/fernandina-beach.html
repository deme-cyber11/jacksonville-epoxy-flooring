Epoxy Flooring in Fernandina Beach, FL

Island-tough epoxy coatings for Amelia Island homes and businesses

Fernandina Beach sits on the northern tip of Amelia Island — a jewel of the Georgia-Florida border, beloved for its Victorian architecture, deep-sea fishing history, and pristine beaches. It's also one of the most demanding environments for concrete in all of Northeast Florida. Amelia Island's position exposes it to salt air from both the Atlantic Ocean and the Amelia River simultaneously, creating a corrosive environment that attacks unprotected concrete year-round. Combine that with Florida's infamous humidity — regularly topping 80% in summer — and you have the perfect argument for professional epoxy flooring.

We travel to Fernandina Beach regularly for both residential and commercial projects. Our experience on Amelia Island has taught us that the salt-air exposure here is meaningfully more intense than even in Jacksonville Beach, requiring an extra step in our prep process: a chloride-ion-resistant barrier primer specifically designed for island properties. This isn't an upsell — it's the difference between a coating that lasts 15+ years and one that starts peeling in three.

Fernandina Beach's commercial sector — boutique hotels, the downtown restaurant and retail district, and the working waterfront — also relies on our commercial epoxy systems for floors that handle heavy foot traffic and frequent wet-mopping without degrading. The non-slip additives we include as standard in all commercial projects are especially relevant in a coastal environment where wet floors are a daily reality.

Our Installation Process

Every Fernandina Beach project includes our island-enhanced 5-step installation:

  1. Surface grinding — Removes salt-compromised surface layer and opens the concrete profile
  2. Chloride-resistant moisture barrier primer — Island-specific primer blocks both water vapor and salt ion penetration
  3. Base coat — High-build epoxy resists the aggressive humidity of Amelia Island's climate
  4. UV-stable decorative topcoat — Won't yellow or chalk in Fernandina Beach's intense Florida sun
  5. 24-hour cure — Light foot traffic after 24 hours; vehicles and full commercial use after 72 hours

Salt air carries chloride ions that penetrate concrete and react with the steel rebar inside, causing corrosion that literally expands the steel and fractures the concrete from within — a process called spalling. On Amelia Island, this process occurs about 3–4x faster than in inland Jacksonville. Epoxy flooring creates a physical barrier that dramatically slows chloride penetration, extending the life of your slab and protecting your home's structural integrity.
