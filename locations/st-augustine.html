Epoxy Floor Coatings Built for St. Augustine

St. Augustine is the nation's oldest city, and its homes and businesses deal with conditions that bare concrete handles poorly. Salt air off the Atlantic and the Matanzas River, humidity that rarely lets up, and sand tracked in from St. Augustine Beach and Vilano Beach all wear on garage floors, patios and shop floors.

An epoxy floor coating seals the slab against moisture, mildew and salt, and it cleans up with a mop. Whether you have a carriage-house garage near the Historic District, a 1950s ranch home in Davis Shores, or a newer three-car garage in one of the subdivisions west of I-95, the right coating turns worn concrete into a floor that holds up for years and looks good doing it.

What Salt Air and Humidity Do to St. Augustine Concrete

The floor that looks "fine enough" is quietly getting worse:

  • Salt scaling. Salt carried on the sea breeze settles into porous concrete. As it dries and crystallizes in the pores, it flakes the surface away, which is why so many beachside garage floors look pitted and dusty.
  • Moisture vapor. Much of St. Augustine sits only a few feet above sea level, and groundwater pushes vapor up through slabs all year. A coating applied without moisture testing and a vapor-blocking primer is the coating that peels.
  • Sand and grit. Beach sand works like sandpaper on bare concrete and on thin coatings. A broadcast flake floor with a hard topcoat stands up to it.
  • Mildew and stains. Damp, porous concrete holds mildew, oil and rust stains. A sealed floor gives them nowhere to go.

Older slabs add their own issues. Homes near the Historic District, Lincolnville, Nelmar Terrace and Fullerwood often have decades-old concrete with uneven spots, old paint and deep staining. Davis Shores and other low-lying parts of Anastasia Island flooded during Hurricanes Matthew and Irma, and some slabs there can still show the white mineral staining that salt water leaves behind. We grind, repair and prime for the slab you actually have, not an ideal one.

How We Handle It From First Call to Finished Floor

  1. Free estimate. We come to you anywhere in St. Augustine, look at the slab, test it for moisture and give you a written quote. The price is in writing before anything starts.
  2. Diamond-grind prep and repairs. Grinding opens the concrete so the coating can bond, and it removes old paint, sealer and the weak, salt-damaged top layer. Cracks and pits are filled, then a moisture-blocking primer goes down. We never skip this step.

Quick Answers Before You Call

How does St. Augustine's coastal humidity affect epoxy flooring?

St. Augustine's proximity to the Atlantic Ocean and the Matanzas River creates consistently high humidity levels that can trap moisture beneath concrete slabs. Before every St. Augustine installation, we perform moisture vapor emission testing and apply a moisture-blocking primer. We also use humidity-tolerant epoxy formulations designed for coastal Florida conditions, ensuring your floor bonds properly and resists delamination for years.

Can you install epoxy flooring in St. Augustine's older historic homes?

Absolutely. Many St. Augustine properties near the historic district and Flagler College have older concrete slabs with unique challenges like uneven surfaces and decades of staining. Our diamond grinding process levels imperfections without damaging the existing structure, and our crack repair techniques restore structural integrity before the epoxy goes down. We have experience working with the specific conditions found in homes built from the early 1900s through the modern era.

How much does an epoxy floor cost in St. Augustine?
