How Much Does Epoxy Flooring Cost in Jacksonville, FL? [2025 Guide]

Published February 2026 | 904 Epoxy Floors

Quick answer: Epoxy flooring in Jacksonville costs $3–$12 per square foot installed, with most residential garage projects totaling $1,500–$5,000 depending on size, system type, and floor condition. Read on for a full breakdown.

If you're considering epoxy flooring in Jacksonville and trying to figure out what it should cost, you've probably already run into the wide range of prices online. The $3–$12 per square foot range isn't a lazy estimate — it genuinely reflects the spectrum from a basic residential garage flake system to a premium metallic showroom floor. This guide breaks it all down so you know exactly what you're paying for.

Epoxy Flooring Cost by Project Type in Jacksonville

Garage Floor Epoxy — $3–$8 per sq ft ($1,500–$3,500 typical)

The most common request we get in Jacksonville is a residential garage floor coating. A standard 2-car garage runs approximately 400–500 square feet. Here's what drives cost at this level:

  • Standard flake system (most popular): $3–$5/sqft — includes diamond grinding, vapor barrier primer, epoxy base, full flake broadcast, and polyaspartic topcoat
  • Solid color epoxy: $3–$4.50/sqft — clean, professional look without the decorative flake
  • Premium flake systems (full broadcast, thicker build): $5–$8/sqft — maximum durability and visual coverage

For a standard 2-car Jacksonville garage: budget $1,500–$2,500 for a quality standard system, $2,500–$4,000 for premium.

Metallic Epoxy — $5–$12 per sq ft ($2,500–$6,000 typical)

Metallic epoxy is the premium option — flowing 3D designs using fine metallic pigments that create a unique, luxury look. It's the most labor-intensive system and uses more expensive materials. In Jacksonville, metallic is most popular in Ponte Vedra, Fleming Island, and Mandarin, where upscale homes call for upscale finishes.

  • Single-color metallic (pearl, silver, champagne): $5–$8/sqft
  • Two-tone metallic with custom swirl: $7–$10/sqft
  • Complex multi-color designs with detailed pattern work: $10–$12/sqft

Commercial Epoxy — $3–$8 per sq ft

Commercial projects vary enormously based on size, system specification, and site conditions. Large warehouse floors (10,000+ sq ft) benefit from economies of scale and often run toward the lower end of the range. Specialty applications (chemical-resistant, anti-static, decorative showroom systems) run higher.

  • Basic warehouse/industrial epoxy: $3–$4.50/sqft
  • Retail/showroom decorative system: $4–$6/sqft
  • Restaurant/commercial kitchen (with cove base): $5–$8/sqft

What Affects Epoxy Flooring Cost in Jacksonville

1. Concrete Condition

The biggest variable in any epoxy quote is what the concrete looks like under the surface. Clean, recently poured slabs (common in new construction areas like Nocatee and Bartram Park) need standard diamond grinding prep. Older slabs with decades of oil, paint, tire marks, or previous failed coatings require additional prep time and materials. Slabs with significant cracking, pitting, or moisture damage may need crack repair compound and additional primer coats. All of this gets identified at your free estimate — there are no surprises on our projects.

2. Moisture Conditions

Jacksonville's subtropical climate creates moisture challenges that most national pricing guides don't account for. Our region averages 75% relative humidity year-round, with summer peaks above 80%. Properties near water — the St. Johns River, Doctor's Lake, coastal areas — have elevated ground moisture that drives vapor upward through slabs continuously. When moisture vapor emission rates (MVER) are high, we specify an additional vapor barrier coat, which adds to both material and labor cost. This is a necessary investment, not an upsell — it's the difference between a coating that lasts 15 years and one that fails in 2.

3. System Type and Number of Coats

More coats = more material = more cost, but also longer lifespan and better performance. A 3-coat system (primer + base coat + topcoat) is standard for residential. High-moisture environments or commercial applications may call for 4 or 5 coats. Premium metallic systems involve more application time and artistic labor.

4. Floor Size

Per-square-foot costs generally decrease as floor size increases. A 1-car garage (200 sq ft) will have a higher per-sqft cost than a 3-car garage (650 sq ft) because setup, equipment, and minimum job costs are spread over a larger area.

5. Accessibility and Layout

Garages with lots of built-in storage, difficult vehicle access, or unusual layouts that require more hand-rolling and cutting-in take more labor time. Open, empty slabs are the most efficient to coat.

The Real Cost of DIY Epoxy in Jacksonville

The big-box store DIY kits look attractive at $150–$300 for a 2-car garage. In theory, that's $0.30–$0.75 per square foot — 10x cheaper than professional installation. In practice, most DIY epoxy jobs in Jacksonville fail within 1–3 years, for three predictable reasons:

Acid etching vs. diamond grinding: DIY kits rely on muriatic acid etching to prepare the concrete surface. Acid etching doesn't create the surface profile that epoxy needs to bond strongly in a humid environment — it only cleans the surface. Professional diamond grinding mechanically opens the concrete pores, creating a surface that epoxy bonds to at the molecular level. In Florida's humidity, the difference between these two prep methods is the difference between a coating that stays bonded and one that delaminates.

No vapor barrier: DIY kits don't include a vapor-blocking primer. In Jacksonville's high-humidity environment, moisture vapor continues pushing upward through the slab after installation, eventually building pressure between the concrete and the coating until it bubbles and peels. This is the single most common reason epoxy floors fail on the First Coast.

Lower-solids formulas: Retail epoxy products typically contain 30–50% solids (the rest is solvent that evaporates). Professional systems use 95–100% solids epoxy, which is significantly denser, thicker, and more moisture-resistant. The cured film thickness of a professional coat is 4–10x that of a DIY coat.

When a DIY floor fails, you face the cost of stripping the old coating (which requires the same diamond grinding equipment professionals use), repairing any moisture damage, and paying for professional installation anyway. Most homeowners spend more on the failed DIY attempt plus professional repair than they would have on professional installation from the start.
